# １　これ　それ　あれ　どれ
when we wanna talk about things we don't know the names of, we use "thing thing" "that one" and so forth. In Japanese, we use *kore*, *sore*, and *are*.
	これはいくらですか　　　*How much is this?*
	それはさんぜんえんです　　　That is 3000 yen
*Kore* refers to a thing that is close to you, the speaker. *Sore* is something that is close to the person you are talking to, and *are* refers to a thing that is neither close to the speaker nor the listener.

*dore* is an expression for which, for example
	どれですか　　　which one is it

# 2 この／その／あの／どの + noun
A slightly more specific *kore*, *sore*, *are*, and *dore* is *kono*, *sono*, *ano*, and *dono* together with a noun. (Note that the *re* series must always stand alone, while the *no* series must always be followed by a noun.) Thus, if you know that the item in your hand is a watch

instead これはいくらいですか　-> you can say このとけいはいくらいですか
*TL: how much is this? -> How much is this watch?*

Similarly if you are talking about a watch that is held by the person you are talking to, you can say:
そのとけいはさんぜんえんです
*TL: That watch is 3000 yen*

And if the watch is far from both the speaker and the listener
あのとけいはさんぜんごひゃくえんです
*TL: That watch over there cost 3500 Yen*
