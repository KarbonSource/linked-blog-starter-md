# Sign Language Dilemmas – what does the Interpreter wear?
https://www.deafumbrella.com/post/2020/02/05/sign-language-dilemmas-what-does-the-interpreter-wear#:~:text=Sign%20language%20interpreters%20with%20fair,to%20wear%20solid%20colour%20tops.

The role of an interpreter can sometimes be seen as an unwelcome addition to the communication process. The interpreter often times becomes the benchmark, and the deaf client will be judged accordingly.

As BSL interpreters and professional Communication Support Workers (CSWs), we know that first impressions matter. How we present ourselves is just as important as how we deliver the interpretation.
- Because the way we dress influences how we are perceived- and, in turn, how our clients are perceived.
- Because our appearance should never be a distraction from the interpretation.
- Because for clients with visual impairments, incorrect clothing choices can make interpretations hard to follow.

Interpreters play a vital role in bridging communication between Deaf and hearing individuals, and their professional appearance and behavior must reflect that.

# Interpreter attire basics -  What to wear
A professional should always dress in a way that is unobtrusive and allows the focus to remain on communication. While black is traditionally taught as the go-to color, Deaf umbrella recommends muted clothes that contrast your skin
- If you have fair skin opt for black, navy blue, dark grey, or olive green.
- If you have darker skin, go for shades of beige, pastel, or cream.

Both men and women should wear long trousers.
Women may wear skirts or dresses, provided they are appropriate length.
Hair should be tidy and away from the face to ensure facial expressions are clearly visible.

# Big No-No's: What not to wear
- Shiny Fabrics
- Busy Patterns
- Short Skirts and dresses with high slits
- Flashy jewelry and loud nail polish
- Casual wear
- Vest tops or low-cut tops
- Unkempt facial hear
- Long or artificial nails

# Dress code adjustments for specific jobs
- Courtrooms and formal meetings - Business attire or a suit
- Hospitals and medical settings - PPE or Scrubs
- Constructions sites - Stout or Steel-toe boots for safety
