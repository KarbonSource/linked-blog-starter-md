# Dummy Hoy: a Deaf Hero
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZuXKVhVphg
Creative Access & Stories R Us productions, one conglomeration of life stories

Dummy Hoy a baseball player for the Red Sox in the early 1900s, he was considered one of the best baseball players in the country.

For 70 years people tried to induct him in the National Baseball Hall of Fame in NY

Back then people called deaf and mutes, dummy.

He was born in Ohio on May 23rd, 1862. At 3 years old William contracted spinal meningitis leaving him deaf.

He would be made fun of by other kids for the sounds he mad trying to speak.

Back then people thought the deaf weren't able to be educated. When William was 10 his parents found out about the Ohio School for the Deaf in Columbus and took him there. At the school William learned to be a cobbler. While making shoes he would be envious of his seniors playing baseball in the school yard. He asked his seniors if he could play, but they just laughed at him and told him he was too skinny to play.

Ed Dundan, William admired how he would throw a baseball, and William would go to a wall and practice throwing. Until one day Ed himself appeared before and told him if you wanna play baseball then you have to be better than everyone else since he's small and deaf

William's father didn't care much about baseball and believed that it wouldn't get William anywhere. William would work for Mr. Beagle to work on people's shoes, but people didn't like the idea of a Dummy working on their shoes.

A group of boys would be playing baseball outside where William worked, and later William and his brothers would join them and form a team called the jackson ball player

A scout saw William play when he was 24 and tried to reach out to him, but upon learning he was deaf was disappointed and continued on. He returned the next day to watch him play and visited him at his work and asked him to play for their team against their rival and William accepted, but needed permission from his parents. His dad was still dismissive.

William joined to scout to prove he was good at basketball no matter the consequences. The scout told him not to tell people he was deaf. Billy Hart is a professional pitcher on the rival team, William gets several hits on him that day. When Billy approached the scout and figured out he lost to a deaf player he was shocked. The scout wanted him to tryout for the Milwaukee team, and his father gave him his blessing.

The Milwaukee manager was not kind to him, he had no patience especially when he found out he was deaf, at least until he saw him throw. the manager offered him a spot on the team, but being lowballed, William declined.

The scout wanted him to go to Oshkosh. He was laughed at by the manager but he was persistent and asked them to give him a chance. Frank Selee was the manager at the time. William did a bold move and said he was better than all the boys on the field and Frank gave him a chance. In 1886 at the age of 24, his professional career began.

Milwaukee owner desperate to get William back at any cost offered him a lot of money, but each offer was declined.

with a batting average of .219 his first year was not the greatest and didn't know what the umpire called, since they didn't use signals back then. On the field however, he was unstoppable.

Frank the manager was under pressure to make a great team and wanted to keep William, but he need to bring his batting average up. William offered to teach Frank sing language and now he just had to look at his third base coach and not turn his head to the umpire.

When it was dark during a game it was hard to see the third base coach, so William came up with a plan to teach the umpires signals for out, strike, etc. and they agreed.

The town of Oshkosh came out to celebrate in honor of William and gave him a walking stick, which he used for the first time when he turned 99

At a deaf school he finds a woman named "Anna Marie" and falls in love with her, also tries to learn to speak from her as she teaches speech.

During his off time when he wasn't playing in majors he would visit Anna

Since William is so good at baseball he feels like he is treated as a man and people forget he is deaf.

Washington wanted him back for the 1889 season. William began reading players body language and could tell exactly where it was heading and get the jump on it.

William threw out 3 runners from center field which was a major accomplishment. 

William has the 25th best batting average in the Red Sox's history, at the time of this documentary.

When William found out he was going to Louisville, he popped the question to Anna, if she would marry him. On October 26th, 1898 they were married.
