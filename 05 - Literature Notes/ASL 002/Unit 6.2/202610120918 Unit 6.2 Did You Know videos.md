# Dummy Hoy: a Deaf Hero
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZuXKVhVphg
Creative Access & Stories R Us productions, one conglomeration of life stories

Dummy Hoy a baseball player for the Red Sox in the early 1900s, he was considered one of the best baseball players in the country.

For 70 years people tried to induct him in the National Baseball Hall of Fame in NY

Back then people called deaf and mutes, dummy.

He was born in Ohio on May 23rd, 1862. At 3 years old William contracted spinal meningitis leaving him deaf.

He would be made fun of by other kids for the sounds he mad trying to speak.

Back then people thought the deaf weren't able to be educated. When William was 10 his parents found out about the Ohio School for the Deaf in Columbus and took him there. At the school William learned to be a cobbler. While making shoes he would be envious of his seniors playing baseball in the school yard. He asked his seniors if he could play, but they just laughed at him and told him he was too skinny to play.

Ed Dundan, William admired how he would throw a baseball, and William would go to a wall and practice throwing. Until one day Ed himself appeared before and told him if you wanna play baseball then you have to be better than everyone else since he's small and deaf

William's father didn't care much about baseball and believed that it wouldn't get William anywhere. William would work for Mr. Beagle to work on people's shoes, but people didn't like the idea of a Dummy working on their shoes
